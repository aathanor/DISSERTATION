Ideas worth keeping but not central to the dissertation's main argument:

- **Quine's essentialism problem.** PRU dissolves rather than solves it: Quine works entirely in $\langle x \rangle$, has no $E\{X\}$ anchor. Footnote in §2.4, not a discussion.
- **Kripke's footnote on scope.** Descriptions achieve scope-independence only in the limiting case where they already function as rigid designators. Footnote in §2.4.
- **$\mathcal{R}$ as projective operator.** Non-commutative order effects in adjective-noun combinations. Flagged in PRU paper; do not touch in dissertation unless an objector pushes.
- **Bourdieu's scholastic fallacy.** Knowledge workers mistake fluent $\langle x \rangle$-navigation for understanding. Relevant to LLM diagnostic.
- **Fictional names as unsaturated patterns.** $Sherlock^c$ with no baptismal $\mathcal{R}(h^o, N^c)$. Worth a paragraph in §5 or footnote on baptism.
- **Large-scope $\iota x\, Fx$ and Kaplan's *dthat*.** Useful in §2 / §3 — the community recognised the gap within its own formal framework.
- **Evans's discriminating conception as misdescribed $\{X\}$.** Footnote in §3.6 — right desideratum, wrong register.

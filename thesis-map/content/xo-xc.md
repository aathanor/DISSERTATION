---
title: "The $x^o$ / $x^c$ Distinction"
kind: "Analytical core"
related: [xo, xc, R, witt, ch4]
---

Two modes of one word — same phonological token, two functional registers:

- $x^o$ (**object-mode**): the word coordinating with an experienced instance — "this $x$ here."
- $x^c$ (**concept-mode**): the word operating in linguistic space — "the $x$," "$x$s are…"

"Dog" pointing at a dog is $dog^o$; "dogs are mammals" is $dog^c$. Developmental evidence (Bloom, Tomasello) supports the two-mode reading.

The distinction does the analytical work for identity, rigidity, and the necessary a posteriori. At $x^o$: identity is nonsense (two objects) or trivial (one object) — Wittgenstein's dilemma is correct for this level. At $x^c$: identity is substantive and contingent — two intensional profiles may or may not converge. This is where discovery happens.

Crucially, $x^o$ is still *language*. Only $E\{X\}$ is experience. $\mathcal{R}$ connects the two sides.

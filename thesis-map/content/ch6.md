**Objective.** Address six objections, ordered roughly by depth.

## 6.1 "Local rigidity is just trivial deixis, not genuine modal rigidity"

The objection: $x^o$ binding to an encountered instance is mere indexicality; it does not deliver Kripkean modal rigidity. Reply: "that bird could have been larger" is a genuine modal claim, and it requires holding *that very bird* fixed across counterfactual variation. Once $bird^o$ coordinates with a particular instance, it tracks the same individual across modal space. The mechanism is continuous with name-rigidity; the difference is scope, not kind.

## 6.2 "Baptism assumes what it needs to explain"

The objection: lifting $h^o$ into $N^c$ presupposes that there is already something to lift. Why does the lift create *persistent* rigidity, when $x^o$ rigidity is itself temporary? Reply: persistence comes from social maintenance, not from anything intrinsic to the baptismal moment. The community treats $\langle N \rangle$ as a tracking device, with norms of correction. Wittgenstein on rule-following is the relevant background.

## 6.3 "This is Mental Files under a different name"

The objection: the $x^o/x^c$ distinction is Recanati's mental files rebranded. Reply: mental files are container metaphors — files hold information. PRU's distinction is not about storage but about modes of word use. $x^o$ is not a stored representation matched against perception; it is the word in its coordinating use. The container architecture is precisely what PRU rejects.

## 6.4 "Surrogates are irrelevant to reference transmission"

The objection: Kripke's causal chain works through testimony alone; manuscripts and portraits add nothing semantic. Reply: surrogates provide *experiential* anchoring, not reference. Reference travels through $\langle N \rangle$. What surrogates do is keep the linguistic pattern partially grounded for speakers who otherwise have only testimony. They are not constitutive of reference but contribute to the cognitive thickness of the practice.

## 6.5 "The formula analysis overstates the case"

The objection: Chapter 2's diagnosis treats Kripke's "object" gloss as an equivocation, but Kripke is aware of the variable/instance/name distinction; the gloss is just idiomatic. Reply: the gloss does idiomatic work, but the inference from formula-validity to name-rigidity requires more than idiom. The metasemantic premise that names behave like variables is substantive and argued for separately (in *Naming and Necessity*); the formula does not establish it. The diagnostic reading shows where the substantive work lives.

## 6.6 "$x^o$ just is Russell's acquaintance renamed"

The objection: dropping the infallibility requirement is not an innovation; it is abandoning what made acquaintance philosophically distinctive. Reply: acquaintance without infallibility is precisely what is needed to do the cognitive work the dissertation requires. Russell needed infallibility for epistemological reasons (foundationalism about knowledge); those reasons are not the dissertation's, and the mechanism survives the change of setting. The renaming is principled, not cosmetic.

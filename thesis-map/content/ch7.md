**Objective.** Reconstruct the argument, identify the contribution, list open questions.

## The Argument Reconstructed

Wittgenstein showed identity is always between designators. Kripke's necessity-of-identity formula captures this at the $x^o$ level, where it is trivially valid. Russell identified the mechanism (acquaintance = $x^o$) and retreated by demanding infallibility. Kripke described the semantic output of the mechanism for ordinary names without supplying a cognitive account. PRU supplies it: $x^o$ as the cognitive primitive (fallible, dynamic), baptism as $\mathcal{R}(h^o, N^c)$ lifting, causal chains realized as testimonial networks with surrogate anchoring.

## What PRU Explains That Others Don't

- The necessity of identity is a consequence of $x^o$-mode designation, not a property of identity itself.
- The necessary *a posteriori* arises because the same identity statement reads necessary at $x^o$ and discoverable at $x^c$.
- The scope-ambiguity-freedom of names is inherited from the demonstrative act at their origin, not stipulated.
- The descriptions-as-rigidified case (large scope, *dthat*) is recognized as derived; rigidity at $x^o$ is prior.

## Open Questions

- Fictional names as unsaturated patterns: $Sherlock^c$ with no baptismal $\mathcal{R}(h^o, N^c)$. The framework predicts that the structure of fictional reference differs from ordinary reference precisely at the lifting step.
- Natural kind terms: the $x^o/x^c$ disambiguation as a research program. The Putnam Twin-Earth literature looks different once $water^o$ and $water^c$ are separated.
- Formal development of $\mathcal{R}$. The PRU framework paper sketches $\mathcal{R}$ as a projective operator with non-commutative properties; full formal treatment is parked.
- The LLM diagnostic: $x^c$ without $x^o$. Whether systems that operate exclusively in $\langle x \rangle$-space without grounding through $\mathcal{R}$ can be said to refer at all.

---
title: "Kripke — extended the scope, didn't supply the mechanism"
kind: "Position in the debate"
related: [russell, pru, gap, baptism, ch3]
---

Kripke broke with descriptivism by treating proper names as rigid designators across all possible worlds. The semantic thesis is well-motivated and largely correct.

What Kripke leaves open: the cognitive story of rigid tracking, the cognitive mechanism of baptism, and how causal chains are psychologically realized. Kripke himself is explicit that he is offering "a better picture, not a theory" — he flags the gap rather than denying it.

Existing post-Kripkean cognitive implementations — Evans's discriminating conception, Recanati's mental files, Devitt's causal-descriptivism — share a representationalist limitation: all retain the template architecture in which a stored content is matched against perception. None explains the cognitive primitive from which rigid tracking emerges.

---
title: "Language can locate, but not hold"
kind: "Central observation"
related: [thesis, xo-xc, kripke]
---

Language by itself is intensional through and through: $\langle x \rangle$ relates words to words, meanings to meanings, inferential roles to inferential roles. Yet language contains devices — demonstratives, indexicals, proper names — whose function is to open onto something extensional.

These devices don't *perform* the connection by themselves. *This* without a gesture and an object is empty. *John* without a baptismal history is a sound. Language provides the slot; the world fills it.

The same observation surfaces across philosophy of language under different names: Russell's distinction between knowledge by acquaintance and knowledge by description; Kripke's separation of rigid designation from descriptive content; the modal failure of definite descriptions; baptism as the device that makes a particular encounter portable across time and speakers. The dissertation takes the Kripke case as its target.

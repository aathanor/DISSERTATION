Baptism is a change in linguistic practice, not a metaphysical operation. It exploits a demonstrative binding to inaugurate a durable name-concept: $\mathcal{R}(h^o, N^c)$.

The crucial point: baptism does not *create* rigidity. It *lifts* primitive rigidity into $N^c$. What shifts is the locus of rigidity, not its nature. Names inherit their scope-ambiguity-free character from the demonstrative act at their origin.

After baptism, later speakers often have no direct experiential contact with the bearer. Their access runs through surrogate constellations $\sigma(N)$ (texts, portraits, inscriptions) and testimonial patterns $\tau(N)$ (archives, curricula). These implement Kripke's causal chains at the cognitive level.

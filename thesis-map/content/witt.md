*Tractatus* 5.5303: "Roughly speaking, to say of two things that they are identical is nonsense, and to say of one thing that it is identical with itself is to say nothing at all."

Russell called this "a destructive criticism from which there seems no escape." Wittgenstein's radical solution: eliminate the identity sign entirely. Identity of reference is expressed by using the same sign, not by a sign for identity.

The core insight: identity is a relation between designators, not between objects. Russell observed it is needed "as between a name and a description or between two descriptions."

The connection to Kripke's necessity-of-identity formula is surprisingly underdeveloped in the secondary literature. Discussions of Wittgenstein and Kripke concentrate on rule-following; the *Tractatus* treatment of identity rarely appears. The *Engaging Kripke with Wittgenstein* volume (2023) does not address it.

At the $x^o$ level, Wittgenstein is right: identity is trivial or nonsensical. At the $x^c$ level, identity is substantive and discoverable. The Kripkean formula straddles both without marking the shift.

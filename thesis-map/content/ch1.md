**Objective.** Open with the framing observation, narrow to Kripke's case, and set up the dissertation's specific contribution.

## 1.1 The Problem: What Kripke Left Open

Kripke broke with descriptivism by treating proper names as rigid designators across all possible worlds. The semantic thesis is well-motivated and largely correct. What the *Naming and Necessity* lectures and the 1971 "Identity and Necessity" paper leave open is the cognitive story: what operation a speaker performs when rigidly tracking an individual across counterfactual variation, what baptism is at the level of cognition, and how causal chains of communication are psychologically realized. The SEP entry on rigid designators describes Kripke's appeal to stipulation as "more like a promissory note than the satisfaction of an explanatory obligation."

## 1.2 Thesis Statement

Rigid designation is the social stabilization of $x^o$-mode tracking across absence. The necessity of identity is a consequence of $x^o$-mode designation, not a property of identity itself. The necessary *a posteriori* arises because the same identity statement is necessary at the $x^o$ level and discoverable at the $x^c$ level. PRU supplies the cognitive mechanism Kripke's apparatus stipulates without explaining.

## 1.3 Methodology and Scope

The argument proceeds by close reading of Kripke's identity formula and its natural-language gloss, then through Russell and Wittgenstein, then through the constructive PRU proposal. The scope is metasemantic: the dissertation does not propose changes to Kripke's model theory; it offers a cognitive account of the preconditions Kripke's semantics presupposes.

## 1.4 Roadmap

Chapter 2 reads Kripke's necessity-of-identity formula and exposes a three-way equivocation on "object." Chapter 3 compresses the Russell–Kripke debate and locates the explanatory gap. Chapter 4 introduces the $x^o/x^c$ distinction and shows what analytical work it does. Chapter 5 develops the PRU account proper: baptism as lifting, causal chains as testimonial networks, PRU as metasemantics. Chapter 6 addresses six objections.

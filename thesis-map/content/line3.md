---
title: "Line 3 — The modal failure of descriptions"
kind: "Convergence: descriptions cannot produce rigidity (received view)"
related: [line1, kripke, ch3, local-rigidity]
---

Descriptions pick out satisfiers world-by-world — they are constitutively world-relative. No accumulation of descriptive conditions can produce cross-world tracking.

The scope problem: $\iota x\, Fx$ relative to $\Box$ has primary and secondary occurrences with different truth conditions. Pulling $\iota x\, Fx$ outside the modal operator (large scope) is itself a form of derived rigidity — the description is evaluated once in the actual world, denotation fixed, modal operator can no longer re-run the sweep.

Kaplan's *dthat* operator makes this explicit: $dthat(\iota x\, Fx)$ takes a description and rigidifies it. Large-scope $\iota x\, Fx$ and $dthat(\iota x\, Fx)$ do the same logical work. *Dthat* is nameification: converting derived rigidity into intrinsic rigidity by stipulation. The community recognized the gap within its own formal framework.

Descriptions approximate rigidity only by becoming name-like. Rigidity is the prior phenomenon.

(Honest framing note: this is the received view in the Kripke–Soames–Recanati literature. It supports a negative claim (descriptions can't supply rigidity), which is necessary background; it does not positively motivate local rigidity.)

title: "Line 1 — Kripke's formula"
kind: "Convergence: extensional validity, intensional silence"

The necessity-of-identity formula $\forall x \forall y (x=y \rightarrow \Box(x=y))$ is valid because variables under assignment are pure extensional pointers. When an assignment maps $x$ and $y$ to the same domain element $d$, the assignment holds fixed across possible worlds. The variables point at $d$ and stay pointing at $d$ — no descriptive content, no intensional profile, nothing to re-evaluate.

A variable under assignment functions exactly like a demonstrative: "this one here," nothing more. The formula's rigidity is demonstrative rigidity in formal dress.

The formula does not establish that names are rigid. It establishes that demonstrative-type reference is rigid. The projection from variables onto names passes through the word "object" in Kripke's gloss — a three-way equivocation between mathematical elements, encountered instances, and named individuals. The formula is valid for the first; the philosophical claim is about the third; the transition is unargued.

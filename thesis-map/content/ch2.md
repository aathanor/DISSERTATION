**Objective.** Starting from Kripke's own necessity-of-identity formula, run through Wittgenstein, expose the extensional/intensional and variable/instance problems, and arrive at the diagnosis that the formula presupposes the designator-mode distinction.

## 2.1 Kripke's Sentence

The natural-language gloss reads: "for every object $x$ and object $y$, if $x$ and $y$ are the same, then it is necessary that $x$ and $y$ are the same object." The word "object" is doing work. It transforms $x$ and $y$ from what they are in the formula — bound variables ranging over a domain under an assignment function — into something that sounds like a particular: a thing in the world, something one might encounter, point to, name.

Rewriting the gloss with a concrete term — "for every object $dog_1$ and object $dog_2$" — exposes the move: someone named two encounters, then discovered the identity. The formula has no resources to represent this discovery; the formula's variables are assigned by a function and rigid by construction. Kripke's insertion of "object" before each variable creates the bridge between formal triviality and philosophical substance.

### The Benjamin Franklin passage

A cleaner exhibit from Kripke's own text. He picks out two individuals by descriptions ("invented bifocals," "first Postmaster General"), then concludes: "$x$ and $y$ are both Benjamin Franklin, and it can certainly be necessary that Benjamin Franklin is identical with himself." The mode-switch is on the page: description-identified instances are replaced by a name, and necessity is read off the name's rigidity. In PRU notation, $\mathcal{R}(man^c_x, bifocals^c)$ vs. $\mathcal{R}(man^o_x, bifocals^c)$ — the shift from $x^c$ to $x^o$ makes the necessity trivial but renders the discovery invisible.

## 2.2 Wittgenstein: Identity Between Objects Is Either Nonsense or Empty

*Tractatus* 5.5303: "Roughly speaking, to say of two things that they are identical is nonsense, and to say of one thing that it is identical with itself is to say nothing at all." Russell called this "a destructive criticism from which there seems no escape." Wittgenstein's radical solution: eliminate the identity sign entirely. Identity of reference is expressed by using the same sign, not by a sign for identity.

The core insight is that identity is a relation between designators, not between objects. Russell makes the same observation: identity is needed "as between a name and a description or between two descriptions." The Wittgenstein–Kripke connection on identity (as distinct from the much-discussed rule-following connection) is essentially unexplored in the secondary literature.

## 2.3 The Formula: Extensional Validity, Intensional Silence

How $\forall x \forall y (x=y \rightarrow \Box(x=y))$ is actually evaluated: exhaustive enumeration of extensional assignments. Variables under assignment are pure extensional pointers, rigid by construction. The formula is trivially valid at the extensional level. It has no resources to address the intensional dimension where Hesperus/Phosphorus lives.

Relation to Schwarz (2006): the present observation is upstream of his circularity diagnosis. Schwarz argues that Kripke's defense of necessity of identity for rigid terms is circular; the present chapter argues something prior — that the formula's validity does not extend to names without an unargued metasemantic premise.

## 2.4 Variables, Instances, Names: The Three-Way Equivocation

Three things are conflated under "object":

- **Variables** — mathematical, rigid by assignment. They carry no encounter-history, no observational profile, no epistemic thickness.
- **Instances** — encountered particulars, rigid within the encounter. Carry perceptual content but only for the duration of the encounter.
- **Names** — socially stabilized, rigid across absence. The category Kripke's argument is ultimately about.

The gloss bridges all three under "object." The formula is valid for the first; the philosophical claim is about the third; the transition passes through the second without being made explicit.

## 2.5 What the Formula Presupposes

The formula is valid because variables are rigid by construction and domain elements are self-identical by set-theoretic axiom. When Kripke applies it to names, the transition requires the substantive thesis that names behave like variables — that they hold their referent fixed across possible worlds. The formula does not establish this. Kripke argues for it independently through modal intuition tests.

The natural-language gloss — with its pivotal word "object" — lends the thesis an air of logical inevitability that belongs only to the extensional level. The formula presupposes what a metasemantic theory is supposed to explain.

Garson (2024) shows that the quantified modal logics that validate the necessity of identity for rigid terms are "very different from ones Kripke developed," with "serious costs whichever solution we attempt." The technical non-triviality matches the metasemantic gap.

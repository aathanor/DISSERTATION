From "Identity and Necessity": Kripke picks out two individuals by descriptions ("invented bifocals," "first Postmaster General"), then says "x and y are both Benjamin Franklin, and it can certainly be necessary that Benjamin Franklin is identical with himself."

The slide caught in Kripke's own text. In PRU notation:

$\mathcal{R}(man^c_x, bifocals^c)$ — some man, from the concept of men, invented bifocals.

$\mathcal{R}(man^c_y, postmaster^c)$ — some man, from the concept of men, was first Postmaster General.

At $x^c$, identity is substantive and contingent. Then Kripke replaces description-identified instances with a name, shifting to:

$\mathcal{R}(man^o_x, bifocals^c)$ — THAT man invented bifocals.

$\mathcal{R}(man^o_y, postmaster^c)$ — THAT man was first Postmaster General.

At $x^o$, identity becomes trivial (same demonstrative pick — says nothing) or nonsensical (different picks — cannot be one). The philosophical content was in the $x^c$ convergence; the necessity falls out only because the designator was changed. The necessity does not come from the identity. It comes from the rigidity of the designator chosen to express it.

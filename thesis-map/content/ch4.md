**Objective.** Introduce the PRU framework through the distinction that does the work. This chapter carries the central analytical weight.

## 4.1 Two Modes of One Word

$x^o$ marks a word coordinating with an experienced instance — "this $x$ here." $x^c$ marks a word operating in linguistic space — "the $x$," "$x$s are…" Same phonological token, two functional registers. Developmental evidence (Bloom, Tomasello): early word use is heavily anchored to encountered instances and only gradually generalizes into description-mode. The token "dog" pointing at a dog is $dog^o$; the same token in "dogs are mammals" is $dog^c$.

This is not two homonymous words. It is one word in two modes. The mode is determined by use, not by lexical entry.

## 4.2 The Distinction Applied to Identity

At $x^o$: identity is either nonsense (two objects — Wittgenstein's first horn) or trivial (one object — Wittgenstein's second horn). Wittgenstein's dilemma is correct for this level. There is nothing for identity to do: you are pointing at one thing, or you are pointing at two different things.

At $x^c$: identity is substantive and contingent. Two intensional profiles may or may not converge. "The morning star is the evening star" is informative at $x^c$ because the two routes — morning observation, evening observation — are distinct intensional structures whose convergence on one object is empirical.

This is the disambiguation Kripke's formula collapses. The formula is read at $x^o$; the philosophical interest lives at $x^c$.

## 4.3 Rigidity as a Property of $x^o$-mode Designation

Rigidity is not an exotic semantic property names happen to have. It is what $x^o$ designation is. A demonstrative carries no descriptive condition that could interact differently with $\Box$ depending on scope position. The tracking itself constitutes the reference. The necessity of identity falls out as a consequence of this mode of designation, not as a property of identity itself.

The *de re / de dicto* distinction is recovered at this level: *de re* predication is modal evaluation after the particular has been fixed ($x^o$ mode); *de dicto* keeps the description live ($x^c$ mode) and re-evaluates world by world. The medieval logicians had the phenomenon under a different label.

## 4.4 Names as the $x^o / x^c$ Interface

$Benjamin Franklin^o$: the man, anchored in demonstrative encounter or its traces. Rigid constitutively. $Benjamin Franklin^c$: the character, anchored in descriptions, properties, stories — what one knows about him. Not rigid.

Kripke's anti-descriptivist argument restated: names track the man ($x^o$), not the character ($x^c$). The name's double life — rigidity from $x^o$, epistemic content from $x^c$ — explains why the same name can be used by speakers with wildly different descriptive beliefs and still refer to the same individual.

"This chair" vs. "the chair": pure $x^o$ vs. description functioning demonstratively in context. The contrast clarifies where Kaplan's *dthat* is needed (to rigidify the second) and where it is not (the first is already rigid).

## 4.5 Local Rigidity as the Primitive Form of Reference

"This chair could have been in another room" has logical form $\Diamond P(\text{this chair})$. No scope ambiguity. *This chair* does not pick out whoever satisfies some condition; it points. The reference is fixed by the perceptual encounter itself.

The inversion of the traditional order: the historical debate took descriptions as the primary model of reference and treated names as a puzzle. Local rigidity reveals the inversion. Rigidity is what reference is at its most primitive level. Descriptions are the secondary case — a workaround for situations where direct tracking is unavailable.

What baptism inherits: the scope-ambiguity-free character of $x^o$ is what names inherit through baptism. Proper names don't exhibit primary/secondary occurrence ambiguity because they trace back to a device that never had that ambiguity — not to a device that had it artificially suppressed.

## 4.6 General Term Rigidity Disambiguated

The literature's confusion about general-term rigidity (Soames, LaPorte, Devitt) diagnosed: $water^o$ (the substance, demonstratively anchored) versus $water^c$ (the kind, descriptively anchored). Different claims, different arguments needed. The Hilary Putnam Twin-Earth case looks different once the mode is specified.

Flagged as exploratory. The full development of $x^o/x^c$ for general terms is a research program; the dissertation argues only that the existing debate is conducted in a register where the distinction is invisible.

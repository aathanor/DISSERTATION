# Central Thesis

Rigid designation is the social stabilization of $x^o$-mode tracking across absence. The necessity of identity is a consequence of $x^o$-mode designation, not a property of identity itself. The necessary a posteriori arises because the same identity statement is necessary at the $x^o$ level and discoverable at the $x^c$ level. PRU provides the cognitive mechanism that produces what Kripke's formal apparatus stipulates.

# The Argument in Seven Steps

**1. Identity is always between designators, not between objects.**
Wittgenstein (Tractatus 5.5303): to say of two things that they are identical is nonsense; to say of one thing that it is identical with itself says nothing. Russell confirms: identity is needed "as between a name and a description or between two descriptions." The formula ∀x∀y(x=y → □(x=y)) is trivially valid because variables are $x^o$-type extensional pointers rigid by construction. _(Ch. 2)_

**2. Kripke's gloss performs an unacknowledged shift between three levels.**
"For every object x and object y" — the word "object" bridges variables (mathematical, rigid by assignment) to instances (encountered, rigid within the encounter) to names (socially stabilized, rigid across absence). The formula is valid for the first; the philosophical claim is about the third; the transition is unargued. Kripke's own Benjamin Franklin passage shows the mode-switch: description-identified instances ($x^c$) are replaced by a name ($N^c$), and necessity is read off the name's rigidity. _(Ch. 2)_

**3. The $x^o$/$x^c$ distinction disambiguates identity, rigidity, and the necessary a posteriori.**
$x^o$ (word coordinating with an instance: "this x here") and $x^c$ (word operating in linguistic space: "the x") are two functional modes of the same word.
- At $x^o$: identity is necessary but trivial (self-identity). Rigidity is constitutive.
- At $x^c$: identity is contingent and discoverable (two profiles may converge). Designation is non-rigid.
- Names operate in both modes: rigidity from $x^o$, epistemic content from $x^c$.
- The necessary a posteriori: necessary at $x^o$, a posteriori at $x^c$.
_(Ch. 4)_

**4. Descriptions fail in modal contexts because they are constitutively $x^c$.**
Descriptions pick out satisfiers world-by-world — constitutively world-relative. The scope problem (primary/secondary occurrence) is the formal expression of this: descriptions can only simulate rigidity positionally. Kaplan's dthat rigidifies descriptions — but has to be invented precisely because descriptions lack rigidity natively. Rigidity is the prior phenomenon; descriptions approximate it. _(Ch. 3)_

**5. Russell identified the right mechanism and retreated.**
Russell's acquaintance = $x^o$. Demonstrative reference under acquaintance is the primitive form of rigid tracking. Russell treated this as a limiting case; PRU treats it as the ground floor. The disanalogy: PRU's grounding is fallible and dynamic where Russell's was infallible and punctual. This makes PRU more plausible, not less. _(Ch. 3, 5)_

**6. Baptism lifts local rigidity into a socially portable name-concept.**
$\mathcal{R}(h^o, N^c)$: the baptismal act exploits a demonstrative binding (constitutively rigid) and inaugurates $N^c$ whose standing function is to preserve that tracking across absence. What shifts is the locus of rigidity, not its nature. Names inherit scope-ambiguity-freedom from the demonstrative act at their origin. Causal chains are testimonial networks sustaining this lifting. _(Ch. 5)_

**7. PRU is metasemantics: it explains how agents come to inhabit Kripkean models.**
PRU does not revise Kripke's formal semantics. It provides the cognitive preconditions: the developmental trajectory from encounter ($x^o$) through baptismal lifting ($N^c$) to socially maintained tracking ($\langle N \rangle$). The formal model captures the output. PRU explains the process. _(Ch. 5)_

# What Is New

- The analysis of Kripke's necessity-of-identity gloss as performing a three-way equivocation on "object" (Ch. 2)
- The connection between Wittgenstein's critique of identity (Tractatus) and Kripke's necessity of identity — unexplored in the literature (Ch. 2)
- The claim that the necessity of identity is a property of $x^o$-mode designation, not of identity itself (Ch. 4)
- The explanation of the necessary a posteriori as straddling two designator modes (Ch. 4)
- The $x^o$/$x^c$ diagnosis of the general-term rigidity debate (Ch. 4, exploratory)
- The man / the character distinction for proper names: $N^o$ vs. $N^c$ (Ch. 4)

- Rigid designation as the correct semantics for names
- Baptism and causal chains as the reference-fixing mechanism
- The anti-descriptivist arguments
